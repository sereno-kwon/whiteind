Brand

White IndustriesPetaluma, California · Since 1978

Making parts
that outlast trends.

White Industries is a bicycle parts maker that Doug White started in 1978 in a spare room of his home in Inverness, Marin County, California. The first product was Peggers, a Velcro band that keeps your trouser leg out of the chain, and for nearly ten years it was the only one.

In the late 1980s the sewing machines gave way to milling machines, and White Industries became a machine shop. Today in Petaluma, Doug's son Alec White leads the company as CEO, designer and engineer, and more than 20 people machine and assemble hubs, cranks, chainrings, bottom brackets, headsets and freewheels in-house.

Machined in Petaluma

Precision bicycle components since 1978.

The Petaluma shop is split into an office, a stock room and a production floor where mills and CNC machines hum without pause. The broacher that cuts the teeth in freehub bodies is a messy job drenched in cutting oil, so everyone takes a turn, Doug and Alec included. When The Radavist visited in 2018, the impression was of an all-hands-on-deck shop.

Before buying a new machine, they take the current process apart first. That is how freehub bodies went from four machines to one. Robot arms take the repetitive work so people can do what people do best. And customer questions, Alec insists, are always answered by a person.

DesignAdapt or die

Alec sums up the company's approach as adapt or die. For nearly 50 years it has kept up with the industry without letting go of simple, serviceable design.

MachiningFrom square stock

Cranks are machined from square aluminum stock through a series of operations. Hub shells and bottom brackets have been drilled and engraved on the same machine for fifteen years.

FinishingPolished first

Every anodized part is polished first, which keeps the colour and finish consistent. Logos are machine-engraved before anodizing.

ServiceBuilt to rebuild

Bearings are Enduro sealed cartridges, and replacement parts and bearing install kits are sold separately. Parts made to be ridden for years and rebuilt.

Founder · Doug White

From machinist to bike shop mechanic to framebuilder, and finally to making parts.

Doug White started out in the late 1960s as a machinist for United Airlines at SFO. In 1970 he moved to Marin County and joined the first class of the College of Marin's industrial arts program, where he met his lifelong collaborator Craig Mitchell. In 1971 he took over Craig's job at Sunshine Bicycle Center in Fairfax, and his career in the bike industry began.

In 1972 he and customer Phil Brown founded Brown & White Cycles and built around 30 frames, and in 1976 he built himself a 20 inch wheel mountain bike for the trails of Inverness. That same year Craig used the same fixtures to build a 26 inch mountain bike frame for Charlie Kelly.

Doug is officially retired but still comes in to program and fix the machines. Alec, who has been in the shop since he was four, is now CEO, designer and engineer, and carries on his father's love of machines and obsession with detail.

02

Hubs

CLD · XMR · T11 · MI5Titanium freehub, 48T engagement

The hub of 1992
is still the benchmark.

In 1992 White Industries released the Ti Cassette rear hub at around 260g, the lightest rear hub on the market, along with the Tracker front hub, whose oversized axle stiffened the flexy suspension forks of the day. The design has changed little in 30 years, and today's hubs carry it on.

Rear hub shells are 6061-T6 aluminum and freehub bodies 6/4 titanium. Three steel pawls with stainless leaf springs engage a 48T drive ring every 7.5°, and four Enduro sealed bearings carry the load.

Titanium freehub

The freehub body is 6/4 titanium. Its teeth are cut in one pass by a broacher that pulls a toothed rod through the ring.

Hubs to Wheels

From hubs to wheels

White Industries also builds complete wheels on its own hubs. In 2023 it acquired the Eugene, Oregon wheel brands Rolf Prima and Astral Cycling, which still run as separate brands.

At Rubi Workshop we choose the hub for your frame's axle standard, rotor mount and drivetrain, then match rims and spokes and build the wheels. For Bromptons and small-wheel bikes we recommend the MET hub.

R30 · G30 · M30 · A3030mm spindle, 2024-T365 aluminum

Cranks carved
from square aluminum.

MR30 cranks are machined from US-sourced 2024-T365 aluminum square stock through a series of operations. Arms and 30mm spindles alike are machined, finished and assembled in Petaluma.

In one corner of the shop is a robot Doug bought from Taylor Guitars around a decade ago. It sat looking for a job until the MR30 cranks arrived, when Doug programmed it himself to polish crank arms. The person who used to polish by hand now runs it.

Square Taper · Road & ENO

Square taper isn't finished yet

Simple, durable and easy to service, square taper cranks are still in the line. The road crank takes VBC 2x chainrings, and the ENO crank is made for 1x TSR rings and single speed builds.

They suit bikes with square taper bottom brackets, such as classic road, touring and single speed mountain bikes, and come in five colours.

04

Freewheels · Chainrings · Bottom Brackets

ENO · VBC · BBSingle speed saved the company

Single speed
saved the company.

When Shimano's XTR arrived in 1991, White Industries worried about closing its doors for the first time since 1978. What saved it was the single speed freewheel and the single speed mountain bike community that embraced it. The ENO freewheel and eccentric ENO hub came out of that era, and White Industries still makes single speed parts today.

Chainrings and bottom brackets are machined in the same shop. VBC chainrings vary the bolt circle so one outer ring takes several inner rings, and the bottom brackets carry 24, 29 and 30mm spindles on Enduro bearings.

05

Headsets

EC · ZS · ISEngraved, then anodized

A line opened
by the next generation.

When The Radavist visited Petaluma in 2018, the newly released headsets were a project Alec had led from the start. The 6061-T6 aluminum cups are machined, machine-engraved, then anodized, and run on Enduro stainless angular contact bearings.

External cup (EC), zero stack (ZS) and integrated (IS) designs combine EC34, EC37, EC44, ZS44, ZS56, IS41 and IS52. A split crown race installs without tools, in black, blue, gold, polished silver, purple, red or stealth black.

Story

1978 ~ 2026Inverness to Petaluma

From a spare room in Inverness
to a two-generation factory.

From a spare room where two sewing machines made trouser bands to a Petaluma factory employing more than 20 people. Over nearly 50 years in which many parts makers disappeared, White Industries has adapted to change while holding on to making things in-house.

1971 ~ 2023Drag →
1971

Sunshine Bicycle Center

Doug joins the bike industry at Sunshine Bicycle Center in Fairfax.

1976

Inverness MTB

He builds a 20 inch wheel mountain bike for the trails of Inverness.

1978

Peggers

White Industries begins with Peggers trouser bands, made on two sewing machines in a spare room. They sell to Raleigh and Schwinn.

1988

First MTB Parts

Sewing machines give way to mills; in Novato the first MTB parts arrive, a sealed titanium spindle BB and the Limbo Spider.

1991

The XTR Pinch

Shimano XTR arrives, and for the first time closing the doors seems possible.

1992

Ti Cassette Hub

The 260g Ti Cassette rear hub, the lightest of its day, and the Tracker front hub. The roots of today's hubs.

1990s ~

ENO · Single Speed

The ENO freewheel and eccentric hub ride the single speed boom and save the company.

2018

Headsets

Headsets led by Alec are released. The Radavist visits the Petaluma shop.

2023

Rolf Prima · Astral

Acquires the Oregon wheel brands Rolf Prima and Astral Cycling.
